# Storytelling pass, 16 Aug 2026

Read every page. This is not a copy-edit, the prose is already good. It is about
structure: what the site sets up, what it pays off, and where the spine has a
hole in it.

## What is working, so it does not get broken

**The setup-and-payoff is real, not decorative.** The grec sandwich is planted in
chapter one with "I have been chasing that sandwich professionally ever since",
flagged in the margin as "this sandwich comes back later", and lands in chapter
five. The empty kitchen from the Tiramisu Response Unit is explicitly handed
forward to chapter six. Very few personal sites do this at all.

**The margin notes are the best device on the site.** They are a commentary
track, and they earn their place because they say things the prose cannot say
about itself: "never launched", "still my best brand name", "told two years
later".

**Failure is on the page.** £26k raised and £0 earned. Grec Bros never launched.
"A lot of people churned because the app stopped giving them the hit they came
for." Killing a venture well is an underrated skill. This is the whole reason the
site reads as trustworthy, and it is rarer than it sounds.

**The craft page is the best-written page.** It is about restraint and it
demonstrates restraint. "If everything is highlighted, nothing is." It also does
the hardest thing in design writing, which is explaining a decision by naming
what was rejected: the Caslon and the Garamond, both binbagged.

## The three real problems

### 1. Goldman Sachs is missing from the spine

The homepage bio says "started at Goldman Sachs at eighteen, placed on the wrong
team". That is the best hook on the site. The chapters page then goes Dièppe,
MiniDeed, JFK, Tiramisu, 140 days, Simply Smashed, the cluster, Thoughtleadr,
Draper. Goldman never gets a chapter. It survives only as a subordinate clause in
chapter eight, "a lunch spot from my Goldman days".

Meanwhile the two highest-performing posts in the feed are both Goldman: the
wrong-team story at 72 reactions, and talking his way onto Mehti's janky side
project at 32. The material is proven and it is not in the spine.

It also creates a gap the reader can feel. He is eighteen and at Goldman, then
the next thing we hear he is a student founding MiniDeed. Chapter 1.5 is missing.

### 2. "The cluster" is a clearing-house, and it buries the best small story

Chapter seven compresses SIMPLYCLO, Peruvian Street Kitchen and Simply Foundation
into two paragraphs. After six chapters of proper storytelling this reads as
admin.

PSK gets one sentence: "Sixteen failed attempts at the green sauce before it
clicked." That is a complete story on its own and there is now evidence for every
beat of it in the archive: a lunch from the Goldman days he could not stop
thinking about, sixteen attempts, a green sauce he then ate with everything for a
month, a Peruvian customer confirming it was legitimate, and the thing becoming
its own brand. It is the same shape as the grec sandwich in chapter one, a food
memory chased for years, which means the site already has a rhyme it is not
using.

### 3. The feed is the least-designed thing on a very designed site

The ventures index has the site's best idea in one line: "Each one shows what I
say about it now next to what I was actually posting at the time, which is not
always the same story."

The homepage feed does none of that. It is reverse chronological, so whatever was
posted last is what a first-time visitor meets. Tonight that is a Goldman story,
which is lucky. Next week it might be the espresso slush.

## Suggestions, ranked by what they buy

1. **Add a Goldman chapter.** Between Dièppe and MiniDeed. The wrong team, and
   Mehti's side project. Both are already written as posts, both performed, and
   it closes the eighteen-to-student gap.

2. **Give PSK its own chapter and cut "the cluster" down.** Let SIMPLYCLO and
   Simply Foundation share a short chapter about what burger money paid for,
   which is a cleaner idea than "cluster" anyway. PSK stands alone and rhymes
   with chapter one.

3. **Let the feed lead with something chosen.** One pinned piece above the
   chronological run, swapped when he likes. The rest can stay as it is.

4. **Move "placed on the wrong team" earlier in the bio.** It is currently the
   fourth clause of the first sentence. It is the most interesting thing in the
   paragraph.

5. **Lift one line out of chapter eight.** "Nobody at the restaurant knew. I was
   taking calls with founders who had just raised, then going back out to the
   pass." That is the strongest single image on the site and it is sitting in the
   middle of a paragraph in the second-to-last chapter.

## One correction applied tonight

Chapter one said Dad ran the Italian restaurant. It now reads that Dad and his
brother ran it, and that he was waiting tables there by sixteen. That is not only
accurate, it fixes a setup problem: the brother previously appeared from nowhere
in chapter four, "me and my brother took it over within the week".
