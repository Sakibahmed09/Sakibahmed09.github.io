I was born in Dièppe, France, moved to east London at eight. Trained as an engineer and started at Goldman Sachs at eighteen, placed on the wrong team.

These days I’m a co-founder at Draper with Mustafa and Aaquib, where we turn founders into industry authorities. Mustafa and I met at an agency and spent two years there on YC founders and a16z-backed companies before we went and did our own version of it. The thesis hasn’t moved since, that content is product and product is content, and Mustafa has asked me to stop saying it.

Before Draper I built Simply Smashed with my brother, out of the back of a flower shop. London’s highest rated burger joint on Google. 4.9 stars across 3,000 reviews, and not a pound of paid marketing behind any of it. He runs it now and runs it better, which I get told regularly.

I also built MiniDeed as a student, a social app where likes became penny donations. It raised £26k for charity across 25 countries and still takes donations daily.

On the side I build small Muslim software, things like dhikry and vibenasheeds and Bilal. I’m a tinkerer more than anything.

I was 18 and got placed on the wrong team at Goldman Sachs. They'd just launched a technology apprenticeship, first cohort ever, and I was supposed to go into a traditional software engineering role but instead I ended up on something called the voice engineering team where the second youngest person was at least double my age. So I just started showing up to things. That's how I ended up sat in rooms watching Amazon and Cisco pitch Goldman on replacing the hoots and the turrets... the hoots are the broadcast intercoms and the turrets are these massive phone setups bolted to every trader's desk. Multimillion pound infrastructure decisions and I'm there acting like I belong. I'm watching pitches fall apart, watching projects get greenlit and then quietly die six months later, and I'm realising the interesting stuff isn't just the engineering it's everything around it. I moved into a proper engineering role eventually. But I think about that stretch a lot because nobody was checking on me, no one would've noticed if I'd just sat at my desk and coasted through it. It would've been the easiest thing in the world to do nothing. I just kept walking into rooms I had no business being in because I wanted to know what was going on.

Sakib Ahmed Co-founder of Draper · Own your distribution · 13 Aug 2026 in

I got 6 A*s and 5 As at GCSE. Then I got BBDD at A-level. Nobody's mentioned the GCSEs since. But I still hear about the A-levels. At 16 I was coasting and I didn't even know it. I thought being smart was a personality trait and not something you actually had to maintain. And A-levels are the moment the system stops rewarding you for being quick and starts asking if you can actually sit down and do the work. I could not sit down and do the work. BBDD hit and everyone knew. That's the thing about public failure when you've been publicly impressive, people do the maths for you. But I think about it now and that was the first time I learned anything useful. Everything I've built since, the restaurant, the agency career, Draper, came from a version of me that got humbled at 17 and finally figured out that showing up when it's boring is the entire game. It's results day today. If your numbers aren't what you expected I'm not going to give you the rejection is redirection speech. I'll just say BBDD was probably the best thing that happened to me tbh.

Sakib Ahmed Co-founder of Draper · Own your distribution · 11 Aug 2026 in

The best thing I did at Goldman Sachs was talk my way onto someone else's janky side project. Mehti prayed next to me every day. One afternoon on the way out I asked how it was going. I meant it as a greeting. He heard it as a question about his startup, and started telling me everything. He'd coded it himself and it barely worked. The idea was that every like turns into a penny for charity, so content creators could fundraise just by posting. I couldn't stop thinking about it. So I asked if I could work on it. I was 19. The whole company was two co-founders, one engineer, and me. We got into an accelerator called Fast Forward in Stratford. My Goldman apprenticeship was three days work, two days uni, so I started skipping the uni days and going to the programme instead. I didn't have a growth strategy. I just started posting about what we were building on Twitter because it felt like the obvious thing to do. Then I found a hook that made people finally get what MiniDeed was, and one tweet went viral. That turned into a funnel into a Telegram community, and it kept growing on its own. Then Ramadan hit. High profile Muslim creators started reaching out, New Arab, Islam Channel. We ran a campaign and the numbers got kind of mad. 26,000 donations, 800,000 taps, 47,000 posts across 26 countries. oh btw - he never did tell me how he was doing.

Sakib Ahmed Co-founder of Draper · Own your distribution · 6 Aug 2026 in

A hacker posted crypto slop from Brian Chesky's account and nobody thought he'd been hacked. They thought he'd started using ChatGPT. We're so deep in the AI content era that a founder posting something that sounds a bit off just reads as normal now. Every post gets filtered through "did they actually write this" before anyone even gets to the idea. The founders I work with who never get that question, their thinking is just so obviously theirs that nobody asks. Nobody reads a post about your chaiwalla in Birmingham or your laptop wedged next to a fryer and wonders if a chatbot came up with it. Chesky's response was perfect btw, "to my new crypto followers: I'm going to be a very disappointing follow." P.S: the hacker didn't even sell anything, just dropped some AI slop and left. could've done that on LinkedIn for free.

Sakib Ahmed Co-founder of Draper · Own your distribution · 30 Jul 2026 in

I keep entering domains the slow way in the fastest era in history. Twice now and I'd do it again. You can go to Claude right now and start building in a domain you have zero expertise in. And it might work tbh, in fact, we're in the most competitive era of software in history because of that. So why would you go into a domain you haven't earned? Go into the one where you've put the most reps in. That's your edge now. We grew Simply Smashed to the highest rated burger in London organically and that whole experience pulled me into writing for founders at agencies. I did that for a year, which led to Draper. With Vibenasheeds same thing. Grew a YouTube channel first, figured out what hit and what didn't, then started building. It reminds me of something Abdel once said to me... AI gets you 60% of the way there, it's on you to fill the gap. That 60% is everything that's ever been publicly scraped. The other 40% doesn't exist in any dataset. You learn it in the arena. P.S: If you want a break from the arena Kotor (it's like a less busy lake como) is one of the most underrated places in Europe, definitely recommend :) .
