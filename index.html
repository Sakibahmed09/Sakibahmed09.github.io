1. On one wordthe prayer room door

Arabic has a word that I don’t think has an equivalent in English, and it’s barakah. Blessing is the usual translation. As I understand it, it’s the thing that makes a small amount go a long way, and whose absence makes a large amount quietly disappear. I mention it because a stranger once tried to teach it to me in a single sentence, and it took me years to work out what he meant.

I was eighteen and in my first week at Goldman Sachs. I asked where I could pray, and they found a Muslim on the floor to walk me down. He pointed at the prayer room door, said “don’t stay too long in this place,” and left.

I stayed five years. Somewhere in the middle of them I noticed that I had the job everyone wanted and somehow less time than anyone, and that I was the only one of my uni friends earning properly and was saving less than all of them. Most of what’s on this site is what I’ve done about that since.

2. On what I’ve done about it

The best thing I did at Goldman was talk my way onto someone else’s side project. The man who prayed next to me every day had coded an app where every like was a penny to charity. One viral tweet later MiniDeed had raised £26k across 25 countries, and it made us exactly £0.

Then my brother spent £100 on a grill and started making burgers in the back of a flower shop. We had no money for marketing, so I did the one thing I’d learned, which was to tell people what we were building and let them watch. Simply Smashed became London’s highest rated burger joint on Google, 4.9 stars across 3,000 reviews, without a pound of paid marketing. Then I did the same thing with a hoodie, documenting it from the first factory visit, and it sold out in three hours. (My brother runs it day to day now, and I still help with the growth.)

It worked, and I had no idea why. So while I was running the highest rated burger joint in London I took a junior, part-time job at an agency that wrote for founders backed by YC and a16z, and I took their calls from a corner of the shop with noise-cancelling headphones on.

That job is where I worked with Mustafa, and Draper is what we left to build. We run LinkedIn for founders and their teams. So far that’s 400M+ views for 100+ founders, from YC and a16z-backed companies to VC funds, and north of £20M in pipeline from the content. If you want us to run yours, book a call.

I still build things at the weekend. Bilal is a prayer-time app that calls you when it’s time to leave for your mosque, not when the prayer starts. It’s on the iPhone, the Mac and the TV in your kitchen, it knows over 24,000 mosques, and it’s free. vibenasheeds is an AI that composes a track and then strips the instruments out.

That’s the short version. The long version starts in a seaside town in France, and it comes with receipts. You can also find me on X and LinkedIn, or by email.
