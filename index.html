I was born in Dièppe, France, and moved to east London at eight. At eighteen Goldman Sachs took me on as an apprentice engineer, first cohort, and put me on the wrong team. I stayed five years.

These days I’m a co-founder at Draper with Mustafa and Aaquib, where we turn founders into industry authorities. Mustafa and I met at an agency and spent two years there on YC founders and a16z-backed companies before we went and did our own version of it. The thesis hasn’t moved since, that content is product and product is content, and Mustafa has asked me to stop saying it.

Before Draper I built Simply Smashed with my brother, out of the back of a flower shop. London’s highest rated burger joint on Google. 4.9 stars across 3,000 reviews, and not a pound of paid marketing behind any of it. He runs it now and runs it better, which I get told regularly.

I also built MiniDeed as a student, a social app where likes became penny donations. It raised £26k for charity across 25 countries and still takes donations daily.

On the side I build small Muslim software. Bilal launched in September, an athan that tells you when to leave for jama’ah at your masjid, not when the prayer starts, on your phone, your Mac and the TV in your kitchen. There is also dhikry, which is invite only, and vibenasheeds. I’m a tinkerer more than anything.

Find me on X and LinkedIn, or by email.

Sakib Ahmed Co-founder of Draper · Own your distribution · 14 Sep 2026 in

"Hey Sakib, we're gonna terminate your contract." I was 22, on £87k, and I could do the job. They walked me out anyway, and it was the one decision I couldn't make myself: The room had a laptop already open on the table and the head of HR sat behind it. Proper American style. Badge, box, door. I had Simply Smashed running the whole time. I'd sit in sprint planning half-listening to a roadmap while working out how many patties we needed for Saturday. Then I'd be round the back of the restaurant between orders, answering Slack threads about product specs off my phone. Two lives at once and not really present in either. I wasn't sleeping. My joints were flaring up from carrying both, and some days I could barely walk to the office. And I could feel it getting worse every week. But you don't walk away from £87k at 22 when you're from where I'm from. That number means the plan is working, even when it very clearly isn't. So I stayed, and gave neither thing what it needed, until someone with a laptop made the call for me. Getting walked out of a job you know you can do is not something you shake off quickly. Especially when part of you already suspected you didn't belong there. I had a lot of imposter syndrome in that job, and being shown the door felt like confirmation. But a few months later I went all in on Simply Smashed, and it became the highest rated burger joint in London. 4.9 stars, three thousand reviews. None of that happens if I'm still half-listening in sprint planning. I'm a good deal more confident now. Most of it came from being made to find out what I could do without a salary telling me. Every bet since, including the company I'm building now, has been bigger than the one before it. Someone had to fire me before I'd let myself go. I'd rather you didn't wait for that.
