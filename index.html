I was born in Dièppe, France, and moved to east London at eight. At eighteen Goldman Sachs took me on as an apprentice engineer, first cohort, and put me on the wrong team. I stayed five years.

These days I’m a co-founder at Draper with Mustafa and Aaquib, where we turn founders into industry authorities. Mustafa and I met at an agency and spent two years there on YC founders and a16z-backed companies before we went and did our own version of it. The thesis hasn’t moved since, that content is product and product is content, and Mustafa has asked me to stop saying it.

Before Draper I built Simply Smashed with my brother, out of the back of a flower shop. London’s highest rated burger joint on Google. 4.9 stars across 3,000 reviews, and not a pound of paid marketing behind any of it. He runs it now and runs it better, which I get told regularly.

I also built MiniDeed as a student, a social app where likes became penny donations. It raised £26k for charity across 25 countries and still takes donations daily.

On the side I build small Muslim software. Bilal launched in September, an athan that tells you when to leave for jama’ah at your masjid, not when the prayer starts, on your phone, your Mac and the TV in your kitchen. There is also dhikry, which is invite only, and vibenasheeds. I’m a tinkerer more than anything.

Find me on X and LinkedIn, or by email.

I want every writer we hire to leave eventually. If they don't, I've probably hired the wrong person. Most companies treat ambition like a flight risk. They screen for loyalty and act betrayed when someone good moves on. I think that's backwards. I left Goldman Sachs to go and sell burgers. I'm not going to hire people and then ask them to stop wanting the same thing. At Draper we look for people who want to build something of their own one day. People building towards something treat a client's problems like practice for their own. They move faster. They care more. So while they're here, they get the real thing. They run the founder call themselves and get paid for every client they take on. If someone learns everything here and leaves to start a company, that's the plan working. You can train skill. You can't teach hunger. P.S. We're hiring content strategists who want to build something of their own one day. Full role in the comments.

Sakib Ahmed Co-founder of Draper · Own your distribution · 23 Sep 2026 in

Last October I fell asleep at the wheel in traffic and drove into the back of the car in front of me. Nobody was hurt. It took me a long time to stop thinking about the version where somebody was. I was setting up Peruvian Street Kitchen at the time, still running the dark kitchen for Simply Smashed, and taking Draper meetings on top of both. Which meant the only window Mustafa and I could find each other in was six, seven in the morning, before service. So I'd take those, work the kitchen through the day, and then pick the rest back up late into the night. Every day, because there was no other way to fit it. I was a bit proud of the hours at the time. That was the tell. Because the thing I'd built only worked as long as I personally never stopped. That isn't a company, that's just me being a bottleneck with a logo on it. And you don't fix a bottleneck by grinding harder, which was the only move I actually knew. So we hired. And we cut the week in half: every client call stacked into Monday and Wednesday, the other three days left completely alone for building. It sounds like nothing. It's the reason there are now whole days where I'm not the thing everyone is waiting on. It's still early and it's still intense. But Draper doesn't run on how much sleep I got anymore. P.S the guy I went into was called Adam. We swapped details there in the traffic and he couldn't have been nicer about it.
