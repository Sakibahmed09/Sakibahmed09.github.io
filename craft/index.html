1. Type

Four typefaces, with distinct jobs. I tried a Caslon, then a Garamond, and binned both because neither sat right next to the sans. A story is not a reason. Sakib Grotesk Regular carries the large titles. Schibsted Grotesk carries the prose, small headings and labels, with a true italic when a word matters.

2. Colour

The colours are warm paper, warm ink and one seal red, and the red is used sparingly so it still means something when it shows up. The only gold on the site appears when you select text, so go on, select this sentence.

one accent, spent carefully

Dark mode is called after Maghrib in the stylesheet. The moon is the same sun with its rays folded away.

3. Motion

The three speeds are named like gears. Swift at 160ms for feedback, settle at 340ms for state, grand at 700ms for entrances. Everything slows down as it lands, the way a real object would. Nothing bounces except the tasbih bead.

tap to race the three gears
swift 160
settle 340
grand 700

Ask for reduced motion and the site sits still, including the race you just ran.

4. The memo

The front page is laid out as a memo, with a To, a Date and a Re, because a memo is what it is. It’s from me, it’s addressed to whoever opened it, wherever and whenever that was, and it’s dated that day.

The subject line is something a stranger said to me when I was eighteen, and I’ve taken it literally. The bottom of every page counts how long you’ve been here, the little door next to the count opens a bit more every minute, and after five minutes it says the line back to you.

5. Doorsthe prayer room, basement

My story keeps turning on doors. A prayer room door, a customer’s front door, an airport that wouldn’t let me through one, a mosque arch left open. So in Chapters every rule runs along the floor to a door, drawn in pencil, and it’s shut, ajar or open depending on how that chapter went. A lit room shows gold through the gap. Lean on one and it tells you which door it was. The shut ones only rattle.

The paper is lit by the sun where you are. It’s peach around sunrise, clearest at noon and gold in the hour before sunset, and only the warmth moves, never the brightness, so the ink reads the same all day. I took that from Bilal, whose sky does the same thing.

6. Receipts

In Chapters, each red number is a receipt, which is a line I posted at the time, and it sits in the margin next to the sentence it backs up. Like this one, from the year I started at Goldman.1

1Remember after GCSEs we all thought we had a bright future that was mad X, 29 Mar 2016

They’re copied out of my own archive by a script, and the script stops if a single word doesn’t match the post. I can get the story wrong. I can’t misquote myself.

7. Small details

On the Bilal page the footer has a clock. It reads your timezone, works out the prayer times on your own machine and tells you what’s next where you are. It never asks where you are and nothing gets sent anywhere. Press t and a tasbih (prayer beads, thirty-three to the ring) turns up in the footer, which came from dhikry, an app I built for my family. ⌘K opens the palette, and two things in it aren’t pages. One of them hires a Boris bike.

try t. go on.

The icon in your tab is the same door as everywhere else, left ajar with the light on. The scribbles in the margins draw themselves in as you reach them, ink first and words after. Links underline in hairline ink and only commit to red when you hover.

8. Colophon

Eight pages on one stylesheet and one script, no framework. The venture pages write themselves from my own archive, so what reaches you is still static files. The fonts live here rather than on Google, subset down to the characters this site actually sets. Nothing asks another origin for anything, which rules out analytics.

Websites are a hobby. The day job is Draper.
