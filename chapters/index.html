01 Dièppe

I was born in Dièppe, northern France. Rural, quiet, and barely a halal takeaway in the whole town. The highlight of my childhood was the drive to Paris to visit my uncle, because Paris meant a grec sandwich from the kebab shops. Happiness on a plate.

this sandwich comes back later

We moved to Tower Hamlets when I was eight. Dad ran an Italian restaurant, Animadi Italia. His signature was the tiramisu. Mine was eating it.

School gave me my first public humbling. 6A* and 5As at GCSE, then BBDD at A-level. I’d practised a Jack Petchey speech in the mirror every night in year 10 after being passed over for the shortlist, and made the regional finals. So I already knew the pattern: start behind, repeat until it works.

02 MiniDeed 2018–2021

My first startup, co-founded as a student. A social app where every like became a penny donation to charity. All of it went to charity, none of it to us, which tells you how good our business model was.

£37 → £2.7k a month

Donations went from £37 a month to £2.7k a month. Twenty-five countries, £26k raised through likes alone. We refused to show follower counts because they felt toxic, and a lot of people churned because the app stopped giving them the hit they came for. You cannot opt out of the attention economy by pretending it isn’t there. That lesson cost me three years and became the foundation of everything I now do at Draper.

The app still takes donations daily. Sadaqah jariyah beats MRR.

03 Twenty-five hours 2019

In January 2019 I flew to New York with my friend Arefin. I was interrogated and held at JFK for twenty-five hours, then refused entry. No reason given. I held the story in for two years, then wrote it up in three parts: the interrogation, the cell, the reflections.

told two years later

It’s why my businesses have never been quiet about who they serve or what they stand for. If Muslim-owned businesses won’t use their voice, who exactly is going to do it for us?

04 The Tiramisu Response Unit 2020

March 2020. My parents were stranded in Bangladesh, Dad high-risk, his restaurant shut. Me and my brother took it over within the week and launched a tiramisu delivery service, free delivery, the two of us doing everything. I named it the Tiramisu Response Unit and I remain proud of that name.

still my best brand name

We ran a pizza programme for the community at the same time, and MiniDeed’s Ramadan campaign broke its own records in the middle of it all. The busiest stretch of my life. The empty kitchen it left behind becomes important in chapter seven.

05 140 days 2021

About to graduate, savings not sustainable, flight abroad already planned. So I set a public challenge: 140 days to launch a food concept before I leave. Authentic grec sandwiches for East London, the first proper French halal kebab in the UK. I mapped my unfair advantages, tested the name by Twitter poll and voicenote, and built it all in public before that was a phrase.

Then I deferred the whole thing and got on the plane. Grec Bros never launched. Everything I learned in those 140 days showed up two years later wearing a different apron.

went to Egypt instead

06 The quiet years 2021–2023

Twenty-two months in Egypt studying Qur’an and Arabic. Zero tweets. Everything Simply Smashed became, the prayer space, the Maghrib jama’ah, the no-music rule, got decided somewhere in that silence.

22 months, 0 tweets

07 Simply Smashed 2021–2026

We launched from the back of a flower shop in 2021, in the kitchen the pandemic left us. By 2023 it was public. By 2025 it was the highest rated burger joint in the UK. 4.9 stars across 3,000 reviews, and not a pound of paid marketing behind any of it.

The stories from that shop are my favourite receipts. The customer whose missing item I delivered myself on a Boris bike in five minutes, and who took the bag without a word. The Messi and Ronaldo lookalikes who turned up on the same night, and each ended up in the Maghrib jama’ah while non-Muslim customers waited respectfully. Order fulfilment going from 50 minutes to 8. The game is the game.

4.9 ★, 3,000 reviews

I left in January 2026. My brother runs it now, and runs it better.

08 The cluster 2022–2025

Around the burgers, a run of smaller bets. Simply Clo raised £15k for Palestine with a hoodie collection made in Bangladesh, then wound down properly when bootstrapping got heavy. Killing a venture well is an underrated skill.

Peruvian Street Kitchen exists because of a lunch spot from my Goldman days I couldn’t stop thinking about. Sixteen failed attempts at the green sauce before it clicked. Simply Foundation, funded by burger profits, has put four huffadh through so far. And one August I got annoyed at phones going off in the khutbah, so I made free phone pouches for mosques. Five signed up in 48 hours. You can just do things.

09 Draper 2025–

In late 2025 I came back to tech. Draper is me, Mustafa and Aaquib turning founders into industry authorities, with content treated as what it actually is now: part of the product.

I’d been writing the thesis in public for two years without naming the company. Building in public is part of the product. Storytelling is the most valuable skill of the AI era. Content compounds alongside the product or the product stays unknown. Now we build the machine that does it properly.

content is product

The competition is a founder paying $1m salaries to learn the space, and a man whose last exit was $780m. Versus me and my two bootstrapped boi cofounders.

We’ll win iA.
